The Lugones procedure is backed by a growing body of multicenter clinical evidence. All key publications are available for download below.

JTCVS
2025
Multicenter Study
The Lugones procedure for scimitar syndrome: a multicenter retrospective study with up to 13.5 years follow-up
Lugones I, García-Hernández A, Belli E, Pozzi M, Sarris GE, et al.
A retrospective analysis of 38 patients across 11 international institutions. No in-hospital deaths, no reoperations, and no pulmonary venous obstructions detected at mid-term follow-up. CT and 4D-flow MRI confirmed wide reconstructed pathways and physiological venous flow patterns.
Download PDF
DOI: 10.1016/j.jtcvs.2025.01.042
J Card Surg
2023
Original Article
Pericardial tunnel technique for partial anomalous pulmonary venous return: long-term outcomes and anatomical adaptations
Lugones I, Peralta C, De Dios AM, Villanueva M.
Long-term follow-up data on the first cohort of patients operated with the pericardial tunnel technique. Serial echocardiography demonstrated no gradient progression across the reconstructed pathway. All patients maintained normal biventricular function.
Download PDF
DOI: 10.1111/jocs.17621
EJCTS
2022
Technical Report
Three surgical variants of the pericardial tunnel technique for scimitar syndrome: operative strategy and anatomical indications
Lugones I, Belli E, Hörer J, Fragata J.
Description of the three variants developed to address complex anatomical presentations: original tunneling, atrial septum repositioning, and interatrial pericardial flap. Operative decision algorithm and representative case series.
Download PDF
DOI: 10.1093/ejcts/ezac318
Ann Thorac Surg
2020
Original Article
Zero-tension repair of partial anomalous pulmonary venous connection: the autologous pericardial tunnel approach
Lugones I, Peralta C, De Dios AM.
Introduction of the pericardial tunnel as an autologous, tension-free conduit for scimitar vein rerouting. Comparison with historical intracardiac baffling outcomes demonstrates significantly lower obstruction rates (0% vs. 46%).
Download PDF
DOI: 10.1016/j.athoracsur.2020.04.081
Pediatr Cardiol
2018
Case Series
Infant scimitar syndrome: early experience with the pericardial tunnel technique in neonates and infants under 12 months
Lugones I, Villanueva M, Peralta C.
Early operative experience in the youngest patient cohort. The pericardial tunnel demonstrated growth potential proportional to somatic growth, with no evidence of conduit narrowing at 5-year follow-up in neonates operated in the first weeks of life.
Download PDF
DOI: 10.1007/s00246-018-1943-2
