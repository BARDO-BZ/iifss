The Syndrome

What is Scimitar
Syndrome?

Scimitar syndrome is a rare heart defect present from birth. In a healthy heart, blood from the lungs flows directly into the left side of the heart. In scimitar syndrome, a vein from the right lung takes a wrong path — draining into the wrong chamber and creating an extra workload on the heart.

The name comes from the curved shape this misrouted vein makes on a chest X-ray — resembling a scimitar, a type of curved sword.

The good news: When repaired with the Lugones procedure, patients achieve excellent outcomes with no reoperations needed.

Scimitar syndrome is characterized by partial anomalous pulmonary venous return from the right lung, most commonly draining into the inferior vena cava. It is frequently associated with right lung hypoplasia, dextroposition of the heart, anomalous systemic arterial supply, and atrial septal defect.

Conventional repair — intracardiac baffling or reimplantation — carries high rates of pulmonary venous obstruction (46% and 63% respectively in large series). The Lugones procedure circumvents this through an autologous pericardial tunnel, achieving wide-lumen, tension-free redirection.

Why classical techniques fail
Intracardiac baffling: 46% obstruction rate
Reimplantation: 63% obstruction rate
Tension on the vein causes scarring and closure
Long tunnel distance leads to kinking

The Procedure

The Pericardial
Tunnel Technique

Instead of constructing a synthetic tube or creating an internal baffle, the Lugones procedure uses the pericardium — the natural sac surrounding the heart — as a ready-made biological tunnel.

The scimitar vein is sutured to the pericardium, which guides blood tension-free into the left atrium. The body's own tissue provides a wide, living conduit that grows with the patient and never scars shut.

01
Identify the anomalous vein
The scimitar vein is identified as it descends toward the inferior vena cava.
02
Open the pericardium
The pericardial sac is opened to create a natural tunnel pathway.
03
Suture vein to pericardium
The vein is sutured to the pericardial wall — no synthetic graft needed.
04
Close and redirect
Blood now flows tension-free through the pericardial tunnel into the left atrium.
